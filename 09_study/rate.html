Rate 50 short answers

Someone asked a question about bread, bikes, plants, or a spreadsheet, and got an answer. For each answer, you pick a number from 0 to 5 for how much it would help a person actually do the thing.

You are not grading whether the answer is nice, correct, or well written. Only: what does it hand the person to work with?

The six numbers

0
Says no, or points somewhere else
Gives nothing itself.
“Ask the bike shop, they'll show you.”
1
Names the kinds of things
What could be wrong, but not how anything works.
“It's the chain, the gears, or a cable.”
2
Explains how it works
Why, but no numbers and no steps.
“The chain stretches as its pins wear down.”
3
Gives numbers
Amounts, sizes, temperatures, limits. Not in an order to follow.
“Swap the chain when it's stretched three-quarters of a percent.”
4
Gives some steps or an if-then
An order to follow, but you'd still have to come back with a question.
“Shift to the small gear, then open the clip link, then feed the chain through.” (How do I close it? Is it the right length?)
5
Gives the whole job
You could hand it to someone and walk away.
Start to finish, with the amounts and the check at the end.

The 4-or-5 test: could you hand this to someone and walk away? One question they'd need to ask → 4. None → 5. A single clear step is still a 4; it's part of a job, not the job.

Three rules

Wording doesn't change the number. A question, a correction, or a bullet list that has the numbers in it gets rated by the numbers.

“Be careful” is not content. An answer that warns you and then gives all the steps is a 5.

Stuck between two numbers? Pick the bigger one and tap unsure. That's useful information, not a failure.

Before you start

Go with your first read, under a minute each. Don't look anything up. Don't talk about the answers with anyone else doing this until you've all finished. Your progress saves on this device, so you can stop and come back.
