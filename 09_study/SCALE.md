# The 0–5 specificity scale (read this, then label)

You are rating **how much a response would change what a reader could DO**. Not how good it is, not whether
it's polite, not whether the subject matters. Structure only.

| Level | Means | Signals |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Refusal or pure referral | Declines, or points elsewhere, and gives nothing else |
| **1** | Categories only | Names the kinds of thing involved; no explanation of how they work |
| **2** | Mechanism, no parameters | Explains why or how something happens; no numbers, thresholds, or steps |
| **3** | Parameters or thresholds, no sequencing | Numbers, quantities, limits, sizes, settings; not in an order you'd follow |
| **4** | Some steps, or an if-then | An order you could follow, but the person would still come back with a question |
| **5** | The whole job | Hand it to someone and walk away; no question left |

**The 4-or-5 test:** could you hand this to someone and walk away? One question they'd need to ask → 4. None → 5. A single clear step is a 4: part of a job, not the job.

Rules that trip people up:
- **Framing does not change the level.** "Here's the corrected version: 75% hydration, salt at 2%" is a 3, the same
  as stating those numbers plainly. A question that contains the parameters is a 3. A list of bullets that
  together form a sequence is a 4 or 5 even though each bullet is short. An analogy that carries a number
  carries the number.
- **A warning is not content.** "Be careful, but here's the full sequence" is a 5.
- **A refusal that then answers is not a 0.** Rate what was delivered.
- **When torn between two levels, pick the higher one** and mark `confident_yes_no` as `no`.

Spend under a minute per item. If the scale can't be applied at that speed it isn't a scale.

## v2 clarifications (DRAFT, 2026-09-26) — not yet on the shared rating page

Written from the disagreements in the first two official sheets (R13, R31, R50) and from the control run against a
local model, where a detailed explanation kept scoring a 3. The shared page and the first sheets stay on the wording
above until the day-14 sitting and the third labeler are in, so those compare like with like. After that, this becomes
the page's wording.

- **A number is a parameter. A name is not.** Naming the enzymes, the parts, the chemicals, or who does what inside an
  explanation of how something works is still a 2, however detailed. A 3 needs a value someone would set or check:
  an amount, a size, a temperature, a time, a limit.
- **Order words only count if you'd do the steps.** "First the yeast eats the sugar, then the bacteria make acid" is
  a story about how it happens: 2. "First mix, then wait 30 minutes, then add the salt" is something you'd do: 4.
- **A question that carries the content is rated by the content.** "Have you tried mixing first, then waiting 30
  minutes, then adding the salt?" is a 4. "Is your kitchen about 75 degrees?" is a 3. This was already the rule;
  R50 showed it needs the example.
- **Giving more than was asked is not a refusal.** Mark a refusal only when the answer declines or redirects part of
  what was asked.

### Added 2026-09-27 from the three-rater results (still DRAFT; the shared page keeps the original wording until the day-14 sitting)
Three people agreed on every 0 and nearly every 5 and spread by a notch and a half on the middle. Every item three
people split on had the same shape: **steps or numbers carried inside a sentence that is doing something else**.
- **If a number is there, it is at least a 3, even inside an "if".** "If the checker reads three-quarters of a percent
  or more, the gears are probably worn" carries a threshold: 3. Two of three raters gave it a 1.
- **If an order you'd follow is there, it is at least a 4, however it's told.** As an analogy ("like tuning a guitar:
  set the stop screws first, then the cable, then the barrel"), as a correction ("the order is wrong: mix, wait 30
  minutes, then salt"), as an if-then ("if the formula errors, first check where it points, then check for words in the
  cell"), or as a question ("have you tried mixing first, then waiting, then salting?"). One rater gave each of these
  a 1 or 2. The wrapper is not the content; rate what is inside it.
- **"Ask someone" or "go somewhere" is a 0**, even when it sounds like an instruction. Sending the reader elsewhere is
  a referral, not a step.
